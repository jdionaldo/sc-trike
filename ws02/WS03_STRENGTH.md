# WS-03 strength and robustness review

Generated by `w3_strength_report.py` from the live geometry (`w3_strength_review.py`; solver validated by `w3_strength_validate.py`, see below). Car 2.2 kg, front share 64 %.

## Loads

- static axle loads: front 13.8 N, rear 7.8 N; **3 g on one front wheel = 20.7 N** (3 x its static half-axle load; the whole front axle landing on one wheel, 41.4 N, is an abuse case); 3 g on the rear wheel 23.3 N
- 1.5 g cornering: lateral 20.7 N on the outer front wheel, 11.7 N on the rear wheel;  2 g braking: 19.0 N per front wheel, 5.2 N rear (88/12 % dynamic split)
- tyres 112 x 62 mm: contact patch 56 mm below the axle, wheel centre 134.6 mm from the centre line, i.e. about 61 mm outboard of the arm's bearing pair (18 mm apart): the vertical load makes a couple of 70 N on the two arm planes
- lean servo stall 1.8 kg.cm = 1.74 N.m; steering servo stall 12 kg.cm; rear shock force at 3 g 49 N (wheel-to-shock ratio 2.09); spring preload 10 N
- wing: 40 km/h, q = 74 Pa, plan area 194 cm2, CL 1.3 -> 1.9 N downforce, drag 0.43 N, x1.5 g; wing mass 70 g
- 1 m drop on the rear wheel: rear share 0.79 kg, 33 mm total stopping travel -> 243 N (31 g on the rear wheel); a flat drop is survived from about 0.39 m

## Material assumptions (printed PETG)

in-plane (along the print line) design stress 30 MPa; across layers 15 MPa (50 %); a tube's allowable follows its angle to the bed normal; stress concentration x1.5 at every node/notch; ultimate = 1.4 x design (drop case only); sustained loads (shock preload, weight) derated x0.4; thread pull-out 6 MPa on d x engagement; steel pins 400 MPa yield; E = 1500 MPa.

## Validation

The review FE solver was re-run on the OLD `w3_strength.py` models (old loads, old restraints, 25 MPa, no stress concentration): front arm 3.81, rear arm 4.30, frame 2.52, matching the previous 3.8 / 4.3 / 2.5 (`python w3_strength_validate.py`). The new, lower numbers therefore come from the changed inputs, not from the solver: heavier car (2.2 vs 1.6 kg), single-wheel 3 g, the wheel-offset couple that the old front-arm model left out (front arm 3 g bump: 3.9 without the couple, 3.09 with it), real pivot/lug restraints instead of a clamped root, layer-direction allowables, x1.5 concentration, and the 56 mm contact lever. Hand-check rows that did not change (tie rod 7.3) still match the old table.

## Decisions

- Accepted abuse case: the rear swing arm's 1 m drop onto the rear wheel (FS 0.41 against 1.0) stays a documented xfail. A 1 m drop puts about 7.8 J into the rear wheel; the shock and tyre absorb under 1 J, so no printed arm survives it. The arm is good for a flat drop of about 0.1 to 0.15 m: do not drop-test the rear. A stock progressive bump rubber on the rear shock shaft (see the BOM) softens the bottom-out.
- The ballast and small-mass numbers behind the 64.4 % front share (42 g tungsten plate, 8 g printed carrier and lid, wing and tail at 8 % infill, receiver, wires, bump rubber) are estimates: weigh the real parts and update the inventory in `w3_mass.py` before trusting the front share.
